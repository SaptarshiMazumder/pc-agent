# Instagram post playbook

What makes a post from our generations feel like a page people follow, not an ad they scroll past.

## The voice

- Follow the brand's `voice` and `tagline` in FACTS. When none is set, write as a **discovery page
  with taste**: someone who finds beautiful things and shares them — warm, a little fantasy and
  wonder, lightly promotional at most. Think of a fair review channel that also has an eye for
  beauty: it points at what is lovely and says where to find it; nobody is paying it.
- It is "things we found", never "my looks" or "what I wore". Never claim the page bought, wore
  or tested anything.
- Never a catalogue. A post that opens by listing products ("an ivory saree, a navy saree and a
  purple outfit") has lost the viewer.

## The hook — the first slide

- One natural line that makes someone stop, about the FINDING, in the page's voice — e.g.
  "3 Durga Puja outfits I found recently". Short (3 to 9 words), concrete, no hashtags, no emoji.
- A number in it counts what the post ACTUALLY shows — three products, "3"; never a number from
  an earlier draft or a plan that had more.
- When the pictures or the notes show an occasion — a Durga Puja pandal, Diwali lamps, a wedding,
  Eid, the monsoon — the hook names it ("3 bracelets I found for Durga Puja"): the occasion is
  why someone stops now.
- It sits low and centred (y about 0.82-0.86 on a carousel, about 0.70 on a Reel, clear of
  Instagram's buttons), in the heading face, large.
- On a clip it is visible from the first frame and fades away after about 2.5-3.5 seconds WHILE
  THE CLIP KEEPS PLAYING. Never freeze or hold the video for text.

## Carousel structure

- Up to 20 slides. Group by product, in the collection's order unless the user says otherwise.
- The pattern that works for fashion and anything worn, per product:
  1. the clip of the model wearing it — the first product's carries the hook; later products
     open with a short line introducing them ("Found this more traditional one", "And for the
     ones who skip tradition — this lehenga"), fading away as the clip plays;
  2. a still of the model — one or two short lines about the PATTERN AND DESIGN: the border, the
     weave, the embroidery, the colour pairing, how it drapes. What the eye sees, not marketing;
  3. the product shot — one quiet line: "Location in caption".
  Use what the collection has: a product with no clip opens on its still; one with no product
  shot ends on the model still with the location line.
- The LAST slide is the sign-off: the last product shot (or the strongest still) where
  "Location in caption" appears first, then moves up (to_y about 0.12 above where it was) and
  STAYS, as the brand's tagline rises gently in below it and stays too. Both are settled by about
  1.5 seconds, then HOLD so people can read them — the slide runs about 7 seconds. Both lines are
  on screen at the end; nothing on the last slide fades away.
- Other products: lead with the clip if there is one (motion stops the scroll), then the detail,
  then the product; for beauty and small things, a close-up of the texture or the label earns
  its own slide.

## Text on slides

- Every slide is full-bleed — the picture or clip fills the frame — and the text sits ON it. Never
  letterboxed or shrunk into a margin. Shapes, panels or badges the design calls for may sit on the
  picture (the user can ask to remove them).
- A slide may carry no words at all when the picture says enough (a product at rest).
- Few words: a slide's text is read in under two seconds. At most two cues per slide.
- Never cover a face or the product's key detail; keep text in the lower third unless the
  picture's subject is low.
- Never put "found at", "styling visual", "AI-generated" or disclaimers on a slide — the caption
  carries the sources and the disclosure. A price on a slide only when the user gave it.
- Heading face for hooks and product lines; label face for small lines like "Location in caption".
- Sizes (share of the height): hook 0.05-0.06, product lines 0.04-0.045, small labels 0.032-0.036
  — smaller than that is hard to read on a phone.

## Clips

- A clip that drags gets sped up (1.2-1.3×); a slow-motion drape can stay at 1×.
- Every clip ends with a 0.4-0.6 s fade so it never cuts off abruptly; trim dead frames at the
  start or end.
- A carousel clip is best 4-10 seconds.

## Reels (one video)

- The same story in one 9:16 video, 15-30 seconds: hook clip first, each product's clip with its
  line, short holds (2-3 s) on the best stills, the sign-off last. Each still runs as video.

## The caption

- Open with the mood, in the page's voice — one or two lines a person would write, e.g. "The
  festive season is a lovely excuse to go looking for beautiful things. A few that caught our
  eye ✨". Never open with a product list.
- Then the credits, numbered to match the order in the post: "01 — <product> · <where it was
  found>". Sources come ONLY from FACTS or the user's notes — never invent a store, a link, a
  price, a fabric or delivery details. When a source is missing, ask for it (questions).
- When a seller lists a product differently from how the pictures show it (an anklet styled as a
  necklace), its credit says so plainly — never pretend the listing is something else.
- A soft close: "Save this for Puja shopping" / "Which one is your pick — 1, 2 or 3?"
- Last, the brand's `caption_disclosure` (FACTS.brand) exactly as written, when it has one.
- 5-10 hashtags, specific to the products and the occasion (#durgapuja #sareelove), not generic
  spam (#love #instagood).

## By kind of product

- Sarees, lehengas, dresses: movement sells — the drape, the pallu, the twirl. Describe border,
  weave, embroidery, colour pairing. Occasion framing (Puja, weddings, Diwali) works.
- Jewellery: close-ups and the piece on skin; describe the stone, the metalwork, how it sits.
- Beauty and skincare: texture, the hand, the label; describe feel and finish, never results or
  claims ("clears acne") the product page does not make.
- Bags, shoes, small gadgets: in use, in hand, at their real size; describe the material and the
  detail that makes it special.
